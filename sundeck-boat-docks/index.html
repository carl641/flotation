Sundeck Boat Docks

Twice the dock, on the same footprint

Traditional sundeck designs offer double the space of a single-tier dock pier and platform.

High-quality Flotation Systems sundecks feature LockDry® aluminum decking for weatherproof security of the lower dock, powder-coated aluminum picket fencing for beauty and safety, and elegant, space-saving cantilevered stairs.

Upstairs is for sun, dinners, and the long view down the lake. Downstairs stays dry and usable — the same square footage doing two jobs at once.

Sundeck boat docks add more than a chapter to your life on the water. They add a whole other story. It begins with the ultimate in secure, protected docking — from there, you write the tale of relaxation and entertainment.

Our sundeck designs welcome a unique selection of enhancements, including swim platforms, shades, seating options, and much more. The options are many, but the choice is simple: Flotation Systems sundecks take waterfront style to a new level.

Configurations

Ways to configure a sundeck

Single or double slips, open or partly covered — the deck above stays yours to plan.

Single slip sundeck

One full-size slip below, full deck above. The most compact way to get a second story on the water.

Double slip sundeck

Traditional side-by-side slips for two full-size watercraft, with the upper deck spanning both.

Slip and offset PWC

One large slip plus an offset personal watercraft slip, which opens a shaded seating area with a view of open water.

Questions

Sundeck dock questions

What is the difference between a sundeck and a sundeck combo?

A sundeck is two open tiers: a full slip below and an open upper deck above it, railed in powder-coated aluminum picket fencing. A sundeck combo keeps that upper deck and adds a sloped dock roof over the slip, plus roughly twenty extra feet of usable dock beyond a covered slip alone.

Put simply, the sundeck is sun everywhere; the combo gives you sun where you want it and shade where you need it, without giving up either the upper deck or a covered slip.

What determines how much space the upper deck has?

The slip layout below sets the footprint above. A single-slip sundeck gives you the most compact second story on the water; a double-slip sundeck carries the deck across two side-by-side slips and gives you appreciably more room upstairs.

How that deck is loaded — furniture, seating, the number of people you plan to host — is engineered into the build rather than picked from a chart. Tell your dealer how you intend to use the deck and it is designed for that from the start.

Can the stairs and access points be customized?

Yes. Cantilevered stairs are standard, carried out over the water so the dock surface below stays clear and the footprint stays small.

How you reach the dock from shore is equally open: top-level or bottom-level entry to the sundeck, or a ramp-to-stairs entry where the dock sits well below the land. Each entry is custom-built to your shoreline profile. See Ramps & Bridges for the spans and entries we build.

What site conditions affect the design?

Water depth and how much the level moves through the year, current, prevailing weather, the height of your bank, and how much shoreline footage you have all shape the dock. The slip entry point in particular is placed to account for current, prevailing weather and property requirements rather than being fixed.

Permit and zoning rules on your water can also limit height, length or footprint. Your dealer handles permits, zoning and access as part of the build, so those constraints are worked out before anything is fabricated.
