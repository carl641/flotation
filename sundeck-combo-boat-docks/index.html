Sundeck Combo Boat Docks

Deck space and dock roof, together. A traditional sundeck with the protection of a dock roof — twenty extra feet of space, covered.

Open deck and covered slip, together

The sundeck combo takes the best of a traditional sundeck and combines it with the protection of a dock roof.

You gain 20 extra feet of dock space plus a sloped dock roof that keeps you, your boat, and all your outdoor items safe from the elements.

Sun where you want it, shade where you need it — without giving up either the upper deck or a covered slip.

And speaking of safety, our sundeck combo docks surpass all national building codes. We craft them from waterproof LockDry® aluminum marine decking and powder coated aluminum fencing, making them as safe and durable as they are attractive.

When you add dock options like lockable gates, corner benches, cable railing and custom waterslides, your sundeck combo boat dock will soon become everyone’s favorite escape on the water.

Configurations

Ways to configure a combo

Where the roof stops and the open deck starts is yours to decide.

Covered slip, open deck

Roof over the slip, sundeck alongside — the classic combination and the most popular layout.

Double slip combo

Two side-by-side slips under roof with the sundeck carried across the top.

Combo with PWC slip

An offset personal watercraft slip tucked in beside the covered slip, with shaded seating.

FAQs

What is the difference between a sundeck and a sundeck combo?

A sundeck is two open tiers: a full slip below and an open upper deck above it, railed in powder-coated aluminum picket fencing. A sundeck combo keeps that upper deck and adds a sloped dock roof over the slip, plus roughly twenty extra feet of usable dock beyond a covered slip alone.

Put simply, the sundeck is sun everywhere; the combo gives you sun where you want it and shade where you need it, without giving up either the upper deck or a covered slip.

What determines how much space the upper deck has?

The slip layout below sets the footprint above, and the combo adds roughly twenty feet of usable dock beyond a covered slip alone. A double-slip combo carries the deck across two side-by-side slips; a combo with an offset PWC slip trades some of that width for a shaded seating area with a view of open water.

How the deck is loaded — furniture, seating, the number of people you plan to host — is engineered into the build rather than picked from a chart. Tell your dealer how you intend to use the deck and it is designed for that from the start.

Can the stairs and access points be customized?

Yes. Cantilevered stairs are standard, carried out over the water so the dock surface stays clear.

Entry from shore is equally open: top-level or bottom-level entry to the deck, or a ramp-to-stairs entry where the dock sits well below the land. Each entry is custom-built to your shoreline profile. See Ramps & Bridges for the spans and entries we build.

What site conditions affect the design?

Water depth and seasonal level change, current, prevailing weather, bank height and available shoreline footage all shape the dock — and on a combo, the roof adds height, which is often the constraint that matters most.

Permit and zoning rules on your water can limit height, length or footprint. Your dealer handles permits, zoning and access as part of the build, so those constraints are settled before fabrication begins.
