Option one

Fixed Docks

Rock-solid stability for steady water levels.

Anchored firmly to steel, concrete, or wood pilings, fixed docks are the masters of wake, waves, and weather. The fixed dock is ideal for water with little tidal or seasonal water level variation, and supports the installation of overhead watercraft lifts.

Flotation Systems has boat dock anchoring systems for every body of water. From shallow to deep water, in strong currents or on placid lakes, we have a dock anchoring system engineered to provide unmatched support.

Steady water levels

Best where tidal or seasonal variation is slight, so the deck height stays right all year.

Overhead lifts

The piling structure carries overhead watercraft lifts that a floating dock cannot.

Masters of the weather

Firmly anchored against wake, waves and weather, in strong currents or on placid lakes.

Option two

Floating Docks

Versatile dock support for variable water levels.

Floating docks are the adaptive solution for diverse shorelines and bodies of water. Our floating boat docks rest on tough sealed floats and can ride out large changes in water level and wakes with ease.

Each Flotation Systems dock is available as a floating dock. To cover a wide variety of shorelines and anchor point access, we offer the floating dock anchoring systems listed below.

Flotation

Boat dock floats and EPS billets

The ultimate in durable dock flotation and support.

The very foundation of every dock is a quality float, and our boat dock floats set the standard for performance and durability. This is no place to cut corners, and we don’t.

Our EPS billets are manufactured by nationally recognized companies. These foam-filled, rotomolded polyethylene encasements meet or exceed all U.S. Army Corps of Engineers regulations, are unaffected by salt water and virtually all chemicals, and are covered by our exclusive limited lifetime warranty.

Billets are attached to the mainframe using 3/8″ stainless steel bolts and 1″ stainless steel fender washers, secured with nylon lock nuts. The result: floats that remain secure under severe conditions, without separation or movement.

Floating docks

Boat dock anchoring system types

Using the correct anchoring system is critical: it provides the core stability of your dock and directly contributes to its longevity and functionality.

  • Cable and Winch with Swivel Pivot Adapter

    A shore-anchored system using cables and a gangway to hold the floating dock at three points — two shore anchors for the cables, and the gangway with swivel pivot adapter. The gangway acts as a stiff-arm, and the pivot lets it move left, right, up and down at the shore anchor, reducing component stress. Ideal for permanent installations where water levels fluctuate yet the dock remains floating.

  • Cable and Winch with Wheel Jacks and T-Stake

    Exchanges the gangway pivot for wheel jacks and an anchoring t-stake. Wheel jacks allow the system where gangway lengths are restricted while still allowing full movement; the t-stake gives the gangway a temporary anchor point while the jacks let the dock chase the water level.

  • Pole-and-Sleeve Anchoring

    Ideal for rougher water. Solid corner poles through aluminum sleeves provide stable support and restrict side-to-side movement. Sleeves run from 6′ to 10′, longer sleeves giving greater lateral support. Adapts to changing water levels up to 15′; not recommended for extremely deep water or widely fluctuating levels.

  • Stand-Offs Anchoring

    Floating docks held securely at the end of a shore-mounted arm. The stand-off system includes a winch and cable set-up, so the arms can be raised to move the dock to deeper water.

  • Stiff-Arm / Outrigger Anchoring

    For narrow lots or water too deep for pole-and-sleeve anchoring. It eliminates the need for cables, supporting the floating dock a fixed distance from shore on a strong outrigger arm.
